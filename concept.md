# functional-safety-hydraulic-braking-summary
Summary of NHTSA Functional Safety Assessment of a Conventional Hydraulic Braking System (ISO 26262)

# Functional Safety Assessment of a Conventional Hydraulic Braking System

## Author
Shikhar Sehgal  
Second Year Mechatronics Engineering Student  

---

In this report, an assessment of functional safety of a generic conventional hydraulic brake system (includes ABS, TCS and ESC) is presented. This study is being undertaken by the Volpe National Transportation Systems Center on behalf of the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) using ISO 26262 — the international standard for functional and electrical automotive safety/functionality — as a foundation during the Concept Phase (CP). The objectives of this study are to specify possible hazards/risks, evaluate the risk management strategies, and create Functional Safety Requirements (FSR's) for the generic BS technologies that will aid in the creation of Driver Assistance Technology (DAT) and Automated Driving System (ADS) AE technologies of today.

---

As vehicles rely more and more on electronic safety control (ESC) for safety-critical functions, the reliability for foundational systems such as braking become essential. Most current braking systems were created before ISO 26262 were created and are therefore known as legacy systems. As a result, these concepts are currently used in shared resources between the Advanced Driver Assistance System (ADAS) and the evolving Automated Driving Systems (ADS), including Automated Lane Centering (ALC) and other driving automation features from a rising level of automation. This report aims to show how the ISO 26262 framework may be implemented retroactively at the conceptual level of a hydraulic braking system.

---

## System Scope

The system analyzed includes the entire system of components responsible for transmitting the braking force generated by the vehicle driver and the electronic control systems to the pads or drums (two variations of brake systems). This includes both electronic and mechanical components, such as:

- Brake pedal, booster, master cylinder, hydraulic lines, and brake pads/drums
- ABS, TCS, and ESC implementation in a brake control module
- Wheel speed, yaw rate, lateral acceleration, brake pedal position, brake pressure, and other sensors
- communicates with other car systems and the driver

---

## ISO 26262 Concept Phase

The report corresponds to the ISO 26262's Concept Phase, which prioritizes risk assessment, hazard identification, and the development of functional safety criteria and safety goals. Since ISO 26262 does not require particular analysis methods, the researchers purposefully use a variety of complimentary methods to increase coverage:

---

## Safety Analysis Methods

### HAZOP (Hazards and Operability Study

utilizes guidewords like "loss," "more than intended," or "wrong timing" to examine errors from intended system functions in order to carefully identify vehicle-level hazards.

### FMEA (Failure Modes and Effects Analysis)

a method that determines how functional failures may spread to dangers at the vehicle level from beginning to end

### STPA (Systems-Theoretic Process Analysis)

a method that uses a control structure to mimic the braking system from start to finish. Unsafe control actions (UCAs) and the contributing factors that may result in hazardous system states are identified by STPA.

---

## Identified Vehicle-Level Hazards

The report also mentions vehicle-level dangers, divided into lateral motion/yaw hazards and longitudinal motion hazards, by combining the findings of the HAZOP and STPA assessments. Examples include:

- Excessive or unintentional vehicle deceleration
- reduction in braking power
- Unintentional yaw or lateral motion
- Wheel lockup-related loss of vehicle stability
- Unintentional vehicle movement or propulsion in the incorrect direction (e.g., rollaway)

The ISO 26262 ASIL approach is then used to evaluate these risks. The findings demonstrate that risks related to braking systems cover the whole ASIL spectrum, including ASIL D, which reflects the potentially fatal consequences of braking malfunctions.

---

## Safety Goals

Eight vehicle-level safety goals were determined from the hazard analysis and ASIL assignment. These safety goals, which include maintaining stability while braking, preventing unintentional braking or loss of brakes, and guaranteeing optimal braking performance, are high-level safety standards that the braking system must meet.

---

## Supporting Safety Analysis

The study uses functional FMEA and STPA Step 2 to do further safety evaluations in support of these safety goals, identifying:

- Failure modes and their consequences
- The causes of unsafe control measures
- Components, sensors, software, and human input interactions

Both conventional failure mechanisms and more complex system-level interactions are captured by this integrated analytical technique.

---

## Functional Safety Concept

The creation of a Functional Safety Concept for the CHB system is one of the report's main outputs. This idea clarifies:

- Safe states (such as switching back to only hydraulic braking in the event that electronic functions malfunction)
- Techniques for alerting drivers (such as messages or indicator lights)
- Strategies for deterioration and fault handling

---

## Functional Safety Requirements

The study mentions 198 functional safety standards based on this idea and the analytical findings. These requirements include topics like the following and are applicable at both the system and component levels:

- Behavior of the brake control module
- Fault detection and sensor plausibility
- Stability of the power supply
- Integrity of communication
- Performance of the mechanical braking subsystem

The criteria show what a strict ISO 26262 concept-phase output for a brake system might look like, although they are illustrative rather than prescriptive and are not meant to be legal demands.

---

## Diagnostics and Validation

Additionally, the report assesses diagnostic coverage, identifying 280 standardized Diagnostic Trouble Codes (DTCs) that are relevant to the CHB system and emphasizing areas that can benefit from more diagnostic capacity. The authors create sample test scenarios that could be utilized to verify brake system behavior under fault conditions based on the safety goals and requirements. These scenarios show how safety objectives could be confirmed in real-world driving scenarios by combining simulated failures with realistic driving conditions.

---

## Conclusions

The study comes to the conclusion that it is both practical and beneficial to apply ISO 26262's Concept Phase to a traditional hydraulic braking system. Among the report's major contributions are:

- Using a variety of complementary techniques to provide an organized approach to functional safety analysis
- Developing a foundational functional safety framework for CHB systems
- Giving specific instances of risks, ASIL assignments, safety objectives, specifications, diagnostics, and test scenarios
- Providing fundamental research data to support upcoming NHTSA initiatives and policy decisions

Overall, the paper highlights how the functional safety of fundamental systems like braking is crucial to the safety of advanced features as cars grow more autonomous. To ensure safe vehicle operation both today and in the future, systematic safety engineering practices must be used, even to older systems.
